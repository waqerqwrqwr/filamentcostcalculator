The order that works

Start with the filament cost, because it uses numbers you already have on a receipt and in the slicer. Once you trust that figure, add machine time and power with the print cost calculator, then apply a margin and your marketplace's fees in the pricing calculator. Going the other way round — picking a price and working backwards — is how people end up defending a number they cannot break down.

Every calculator on the site takes the same approach: it uses your inputs, shows the formula it applied, and labels its own defaults as examples. If a result disagrees with your gut, the input is almost always the reason, and every input is editable.
