How these calculators are built

Four rules that decide what is on every page. They are the reason the numbers here can be checked instead of trusted.

The formula is printed on the page

Each calculator shows the arithmetic it uses, in plain text, directly above the result. If a figure looks wrong you can work out why without filing a support ticket.

Defaults are labelled as assumptions

A $22 spool and a 12% waste rate are plausible starting examples, not market averages we claim to have measured. Filament prices, tariffs and platform fees move, so anything you did not type yourself is an example.

Nothing is uploaded

The arithmetic runs in your browser. There is no account, no server round trip and no analytics on the input fields. You can open a calculator, disconnect from the network and still use it.

Sources instead of invented numbers

Where a figure depends on a supplier, a utility tariff or a marketplace fee, the page says so and points you at the official source rather than guessing a number and presenting it as fact.

What these calculators are not

They are arithmetic with editable inputs, not a market price list. Every default is a starting assumption you are expected to overwrite with your own receipt, your own electricity tariff and your own slicer estimate. Treat any figure you did not enter yourself as an example.
