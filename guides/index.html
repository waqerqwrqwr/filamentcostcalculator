How to read these guides

Every calculator on this site makes a simplification. The guides are where those simplifications get explained — read them in order if you are costing prints for the first time, or dip into one when a number on a calculator looks wrong.

The five lines that decide what a print costs

Almost every costing argument comes down to the same five lines. Four of them are easy to get right once you know where the number should come from, and the fifth is the one people quietly set to zero.

LineWhat it coversWhere your number comes fromWhat people get wrong
MaterialFilament actually consumed, waste includedSlicer estimate × (1 + waste %)Dividing by the gross shipping weight of the box
Machine timeHours the printer is occupied and cannot do anything elseSlicer time estimate, in hoursTreating the printer as free because it is already paid for
PowerKilowatt-hours × your electricity tariffPrinter draw (kW) × hours × tariffAssuming 100 W for an enclosed or heated-chamber machine
FailuresSpools and machine hours lost to prints that did not workYour own failure rate over the last monthSetting it to zero because the last print worked
Labour & feesPacking, post-processing, platform and payment cutsYour hourly rate and the published fee scheduleForgetting the processor fee is charged on top of the platform fee

The input that is usually wrong

Waste is the line where estimates and reality disagree most often. A well-tuned printer on a familiar material loses somewhere between 8% and 15% of what the slicer says the model needs — purge lines, a brim, supports, the tail left on a spool that the extruder cannot reach, and the occasional restart. While you are still dialling in a new filament that figure is easily double.

Slicer estimates describe the part. The spool describes everything that got spent getting you the part. If both numbers exist and they disagree, the spool is right.

What these guides do not cover

They do not cover design time, sales tax, outbound shipping, packaging cost or marketing. Those belong in a pricing decision, not in a per-part cost, and mixing them together is how a price ends up looking defensible while the job still loses money. They also do not give you market rates: filament prices vary by country, shipping and season, and no figure on this site is a quoted price.

Where a number genuinely depends on an outside source — a supplier, a utility tariff, a marketplace fee schedule — the page says so and tells you to check the source rather than printing a figure we cannot verify.
